Lesson 1 of 52 min read

How scammers think

Most scams don't hack your phone. They hack your emotions. Scammers rely on four triggers: fear ("your account will be blocked"), greed ("you won a prize"), urgency ("act in 10 minutes") and trust (pretending to be family, a bank or the police).

When a message makes you panic or get excited, and it also asks you to do something, such as click, pay, share a code or install an app, that combination is the warning sign. The feeling is the trap.

Key takeaways

  • Strong emotion plus a request means stop
  • Scammers pretend to be someone you already trust
  • Pausing for one minute defeats most scams
Practice check

A message makes you panic and asks you to click a link. What should you do first?

Strong emotion plus a request is the warning sign. Pause and verify using the official app, website or a number you already trust.

Lesson 2 of 52 min read

Spotting phishing messages

Phishing is a fake message that pretends to come from someone you trust, so you click a link or share details. It arrives by email, SMS, WhatsApp or social media.

Check three things before you act. The sender: look for small spelling tricks such as amaz0n-secure.co. The link: short links and unfamiliar domains are red flags. The request: real companies don't ask for passwords, OTPs or PINs by message. When in doubt, open the official app or website yourself instead of using the link.

Key takeaways

  • Check the sender, the link and the request
  • Never share an OTP, PIN or password
  • Use the official app, not the link in a message
Practice check

Which combination should you check before trusting a message?

Scammers copy logos easily. The sender address, the real link and what they ask you to do reveal the scam.

Lesson 3 of 52 min read

UPI and QR code safety

Your UPI PIN is only for sending money. You never need to enter it, or scan a QR code, to receive money. A common scam on marketplaces is a "buyer" who sends a QR code to "pay" you. Scanning it and entering your PIN sends money to them.

Before you approve any UPI request, read the screen: it says Pay, not Receive. Ignore collect requests from people you don't know, and never install screen-sharing apps because a caller asked you to.

Key takeaways

  • PIN means money is leaving your account
  • Read every UPI screen before approving
  • Never install apps because a caller asked
Practice check

A buyer sends a QR code so you can 'receive' ₹5,000. What happens if you scan it and enter your UPI PIN?

A UPI PIN only authorises sending money. You never need a PIN or QR scan to receive money.

Lesson 4 of 52 min read

Protecting your accounts

Use a different password for every important account, because one leaked password can unlock all the others. A passphrase of four random words with a number and a symbol is long, easy to remember and hard to guess. A password manager can remember the rest for you.

Turn on 2-step verification on email, WhatsApp, Instagram and banking. An authenticator app or a passkey is stronger than an SMS code. You can check whether your email appeared in a known data breach at haveibeenpwned.com.

Key takeaways

  • One account, one password
  • Turn on 2-step verification everywhere
  • Passkeys and authenticator apps beat SMS codes
Practice check

Which is the strongest way to protect an important account?

A unique password stops one leak unlocking everything, and 2-step verification blocks someone who has only your password.

Lesson 5 of 52 min read

If you've been scammed

Act within the first hour, often called the golden hour. In India, call the national cyber crime helpline 1930 and file a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in. Then call your bank to block your card, UPI or account.

Save evidence: screenshots, phone numbers, transaction IDs and profile links. Don't delete chats. You can report fraud calls and messages on Sanchar Saathi. If someone is blackmailing you, don't pay. Paying never ends it.

Key takeaways

  • Call 1930 and report at cybercrime.gov.in
  • Block your bank card or UPI immediately
  • Keep all evidence and never pay blackmailers
Practice check

You've just lost money to a scam in India. What should you do first?

Act in the golden hour: call 1930, report online, call your bank and keep all evidence.
