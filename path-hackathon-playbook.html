Lesson 1 of 42 min read

Picking the problem

Strong hackathon ideas solve a real, specific problem for a clear group of people. Read the judging criteria first and choose a problem that fits them.

Write the problem in one sentence: who has it, why it hurts and how your solution helps. If you can't explain it simply, narrow it down.

Key takeaways

  • Solve a real, specific problem
  • Read the judging criteria first
  • Explain it in one sentence
Practice check

What makes a strong hackathon idea?

Clear, specific problems that match the judging criteria score best.

Lesson 2 of 42 min read

Building the team

A small team of three or four works best. Cover the key roles: building the product, design and user experience, and the story and presentation.

Agree on the plan in the first hour, split the work clearly and check in every few hours so nobody is blocked.

Key takeaways

  • Keep the team small
  • Cover build, design and pitch
  • Check in often
Practice check

What team size usually works best?

A small team of three or four can cover building, design and the pitch.

Lesson 3 of 42 min read

Shipping the MVP

A minimum viable product (MVP) is the smallest version that shows your idea working. Build one complete user journey end to end instead of many half-finished features.

Use tools and services you already know, fake what isn't essential, and stop adding features a few hours before the deadline so you have time to test.

Key takeaways

  • One working journey beats many partial features
  • Use familiar tools
  • Stop building early enough to test
Practice check

What should an MVP focus on?

One working journey shows the idea better than many partial features.

Lesson 4 of 42 min read

Demo and pitch

Open with the problem and why it matters, show the working demo, then explain the impact and what you'd build next. Keep it short and practise it several times.

Record a backup video of your demo in case something breaks live, and be ready for questions about how it works.

Key takeaways

  • Problem, demo, impact, next steps
  • Rehearse more than once
  • Always have a backup video
Practice check

What's the best safety net for a live demo?

A backup video keeps your pitch on track if something fails live.
