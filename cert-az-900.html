Section 1

What the exam covers

The exam is split into skill areas. The percentage shows roughly how much of the exam each area makes up, so spend your time to match.

Describe cloud concepts25–30%
  • Cloud computing, shared responsibility, public/private/hybrid models
  • Consumption-based model, pricing models and serverless
  • Benefits: high availability, scalability, reliability, security, manageability
  • IaaS, PaaS and SaaS, with use cases
Describe Azure architecture and services35–40%
  • Regions, region pairs, sovereign regions, availability zones and datacenters
  • Resources, resource groups, subscriptions and management groups
  • Compute (VMs, scale sets, containers, functions, Azure Virtual Desktop) and networking (VNets, peering, DNS, VPN Gateway, ExpressRoute)
  • Storage services, tiers, redundancy and migration tools
  • Microsoft Entra ID, SSO, MFA, passwordless, Conditional Access, RBAC, Zero Trust, defense in depth, Defender for Cloud
Describe Azure management and governance30–35%
  • Cost factors, pricing calculator, cost management and tags
  • Microsoft Purview, Azure Policy and resource locks
  • Portal, Cloud Shell, CLI, PowerShell, Azure Arc, IaC, ARM and ARM templates
  • Azure Advisor, Service Health and Azure Monitor (Log Analytics, alerts, Application Insights)
Section 2

Key concepts, explained simply

These are the ideas that come up again and again. Open each one and make sure you could explain it to a friend.

Shared responsibility

In the cloud, security is shared. The provider always secures the physical datacenter, network and hosts. You are always responsible for your data, accounts and identities. Everything in between depends on the service: with IaaS you manage the operating system; with SaaS the provider manages almost everything.

Scale up vs scale out

Scaling up (vertical) means a bigger machine, such as more CPU or memory. Scaling out (horizontal) means more machines of the same size. Elasticity means scaling automatically as demand changes.

The Azure hierarchy

Management groups contain subscriptions, subscriptions contain resource groups, and resource groups contain resources. Policies and access set at a higher level are inherited by everything below it.

Storage redundancy

LRS keeps three copies in one datacenter. ZRS spreads copies across availability zones in one region. GRS and GZRS also copy data to a paired region for protection against a regional outage.

Authentication vs authorisation

Authentication proves who you are (Microsoft Entra ID, MFA). Authorisation decides what you can do (Azure RBAC). Conditional Access adds rules such as requiring MFA from unknown locations.

Governance tools

Azure Policy enforces rules (for example, only allowed regions). Resource locks prevent accidental deletion or changes. Tags label resources for cost tracking and organisation.

Section 3

Study plan

About an hour a day is enough. Adjust the pace to your experience.

  1. Week 1Cloud concepts: models, benefits, service types. Complete the matching Microsoft Learn modules.
  2. Week 2Azure architecture, compute and networking. Create a free account and deploy a small VM and a web app.
  3. Week 3Storage, identity and security. Practise with storage accounts and Entra ID users and roles.
  4. Week 4Management and governance, then take the free practice assessment until you score 80%+.
Section 4

Exam tips

  • Read every question twice. Many answers are “which service fits this scenario”.
  • Use the free practice assessment on Microsoft Learn until you score 80% or more.
  • Hands-on practice beats memorising: build what you read about in a free account.
  • Skip hard questions and come back. Unanswered questions score zero, so answer everything.
  • Check the official study guide a week before your exam in case the skills changed.
